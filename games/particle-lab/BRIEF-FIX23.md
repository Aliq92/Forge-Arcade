Remove the existing gameplay based on free-floating particles and gravitational influence wells.

Specifically eliminate:

- Attractor wells
- Repulsor wells
- Vortex wells
- Rainbow Rebirth Well
- Other gravity-well variants
- Old well-strength controls
- Obsolete well-placement mechanics
- Previous free-floating particle presets

**Important distinction:** The new materials may be simulated internally using cellular particles or grid cells. The goal is to eliminate the old gameplay, not prohibit particle-based numerical techniques.

Do not leave disabled legacy controls or broken references.

# 3. NEW CORE GAMEPLAY

Create a two-dimensional material-physics sandbox inspired by falling-sand simulations.

Players should be able to:

1. Choose materials.
2. Paint or place materials directly into the environment.
3. Observe gravity, movement, flow, collisions, and reactions.
4. Construct barriers and containers.
5. Manipulate temperature and material interactions.
6. Complete physics-based puzzle objectives.
7. Experiment freely in Sandbox Mode.

The simulation must feel interactive, responsive, and visually satisfying.

Use a cellular automaton, grid-based simulation, or another appropriate deterministic numerical method.

Favor stable and understandable behavior over computationally expensive realism.

# 4. MATERIAL SYSTEM

Implement the following materials with distinct physical properties.

| Material | Primary Behavior |
|---|---|
| Sand | Falls, piles up, and slides |
| Water | Flows, pools, and extinguishes fire |
| Stone | Solid, stationary structural barrier |
| Metal | Durable solid that conducts heat |
| Wood | Solid, combustible construction material |
| Oil | Flammable liquid that floats on water |
| Lava | Hot, slow-moving liquid that ignites combustibles |
| Ice | Solid that melts into water |
| Steam | Gas that rises and can condense |

### Material Requirements

Each material should define relevant properties such as:

- Density or movement priority
- State: powder, liquid, solid, or gas
- Temperature
- Heat conductivity
- Melting or boiling threshold
- Flammability
- Colour and rendering style
- Reaction rules

Use a data-driven material registry.

Avoid scattered hard-coded conditions wherever possible.

### Additional Supporting Materials

Add these when necessary to create meaningful mechanics:

**Fire:** A temporary reactive effect that ignites wood and oil, generates heat, and eventually burns out.

**Smoke:** A rising gas generated by combustion.

**Empty/Air:** Unoccupied grid cells needed for fluid and gas movement.

Optional advanced materials may include glass, acid-resistant barriers, or cooling elements, but only if supported by clear gameplay purposes.

Do not add meaningless materials merely to increase the material count.

# 5. MATERIAL PHYSICS AND REACTIONS

Implement consistent simplified physics.

### Gravity and Movement

- Sand falls and forms natural-looking piles.
- Water flows sideways when blocked below.
- Oil flows and generally stays above water.
- Steam rises and spreads.
- Lava moves slower than water.
- Static solids remain fixed unless explicitly changed by game tools.

### Heat and Temperature

Create a lightweight local temperature system.

Expected interactions:

- Fire heats nearby cells.
- Lava heats nearby materials.
- Ice absorbs heat and melts into water.
- Heated water can become steam.
- Cooled steam can condense into water.
- Lava cools into stone under suitable conditions.
- Wood and oil can ignite under sufficient heat.

### Reactions

Support predictable interactions such as:

1. Water + sufficiently hot lava → steam generation and gradual lava cooling.
2. Ice + heat → water.
3. Water + sufficient heat → steam.
4. Steam + cooling → water.
5. Wood + fire → combustion and smoke.
6. Oil + ignition → burning oil and heat release.

These are simplified gameplay models, not exact chemical simulations.

Avoid material duplication, uncontrolled chain reactions, and inconsistent mass handling.

Implement sensible conservation rules where practical, while allowing clearly defined transformations and combustion effects.

# 7. CHALLENGE MODE

Introduce a dedicated Challenge Mode with **12 handcrafted levels**.

Each level must have:

- A clear objective.
- A designed starting environment.
- A limited or defined selection of materials.
- A measurable success condition.
- A reset/retry option.
- A completion screen.
- Optional star-based performance evaluation.

The challenges must be playable and solvable with the actual simulation rules.

## LEVEL DESIGN

### Level 1 — Sandfall
Guide falling sand into a designated collection zone.

Teach gravity and material placement.

### Level 2 — Waterworks
Build a simple channel to guide water into a tank.

Teach liquid flow and barrier placement.

### Level 3 — The Dam
Contain water behind a player-built barrier for a specified period.

Teach structural containment.

### Level 4 — Icebreaker
Use a heat source to melt ice and direct the resulting water into a collection area.

Teach temperature transformations.

### Level 5 — Steam Lift
Heat water to create steam and guide it into an elevated chamber.

Teach gas movement.

### Level 6 — Oil and Water
Separate a mixed pool of oil and water into designated collection areas.

Teach density and selective flow.

### Level 7 — Firebreak
Protect a wooden structure from an advancing fire using available materials.

Teach combustion and fire containment.

### Level 8 — Lava Crossing
Construct a protected route that allows a target material to cross a lava hazard.

Teach thermal resistance.

### Level 9 — Cooling Chamber
Cool a controlled amount of lava into stone within a designated construction zone.

Teach material transformation.

### Level 10 — Pressure Path
Guide a rising gas through a narrow obstacle course.

Teach gas flow and containment.

### Level 11 — Chain Reaction
Complete a multi-step sequence involving heating, melting, water flow, and steam generation.

Teach combined material interactions.

### Level 12 — The Grand Laboratory
Solve a larger environmental puzzle combining several materials and reactions.

Provide multiple viable solutions where practical.

**Design requirement:** Test every level. Do not treat plausible-looking level layouts as proof they can be completed.

# 12. ENGINE PERFORMANCE

The simulation must remain stable on typical desktop and mobile browsers.

Implement:

- Efficient grid updates.
- Double buffering or carefully managed in-place updates.
- Directional update ordering that avoids persistent movement bias.
- Active-cell or dirty-region optimizations where useful.
- Frame-time limits.
- Controlled reaction propagation.
- Memory-efficient material data representation.
- Responsive canvas scaling.

Make the simulation resolution independent of display resolution.

Avoid unexpected material movement when the game is paused.

Keep simulation behavior reasonably consistent across different refresh rates.

# 13. SAFETY AND EDGE CASES

Handle:

- Empty simulation grids.
- Large amounts of water or sand.
- Many simultaneous fire reactions.
- Repeated rapid reset.
- Resizing during gameplay.
- Mobile rotation.
- Touch dragging.
- Switching modes during simulation.
- Loading old or malformed save data.
- Destroyed objects and event listener cleanup.

No runaway event loops, NaN propagation, or browser freezes.
